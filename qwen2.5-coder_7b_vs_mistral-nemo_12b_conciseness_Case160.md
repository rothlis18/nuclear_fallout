### Key Factors to Consider:

1. **Chronic Radiation-Induced DNA Damage:**
   - **DNA Damage:** Ionizing radiation causes double-strand breaks in DNA, leading to mutations and potentially cancer. The severity of DNA damage depends on the dose and duration of exposure.
   - **Mutation Rates:** Higher radiation exposure increases the mutation rate, which can lead to genetic diversity but also to increased susceptibility to diseases and reduced fitness.

2. **Shortened Individual Lifespans:**
   - **Increased Mortality:** Chronic radiation exposure leads to increased mortality rates due to radiation-induced cancers, genetic disorders, and other radiation-related illnesses.
   - **Reduced Lifespan:** The average lifespan of wildlife in these zones is significantly shorter compared to unaffected areas.

3. **Elevated Mutation Rates:**
   - **Genetic Diversity:** While increased mutation rates can lead to genetic diversity, this diversity can also be detrimental if not managed properly.
   - **Adaptation:** Some species may develop adaptations to cope with radiation, such as increased resistance to certain types of cancer.

4. **Reduced Anthropogenic Pressures:**
   - **Habitat Fragmentation:** Reduced human activity leads to less habitat fragmentation, which can increase the availability of contiguous habitats for wildlife.
   - **Agriculture:** Reduced agricultural activity means less land use for crops and livestock, potentially leading to more natural habitats.
   - **Hunting:** Reduced hunting pressure allows wildlife populations to grow without interference.
   - **Industrial Pollution:** Decreased industrial pollution reduces the overall environmental stress on wildlife.
