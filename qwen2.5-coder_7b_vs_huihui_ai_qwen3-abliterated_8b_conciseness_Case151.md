Wildlife thrives in Chernobyl and Fukushima exclusion zones due to reduced anthropogenic pressures, which outweigh the fitness penalties of chronic radiation exposure. Habitat fragmentation, agriculture, hunting, and industrial pollution are absent, allowing species to thrive without human interference. The net reproductive survival of wildlife in these zones is higher than in comparable, undisturbed habitats. The trade-off between radiation-induced DNA damage, shortened lifespans, and elevated mutation rates is mitigated by the absence of human-induced mortality. This assessment highlights the importance of conservation and management strategies that prioritize reducing human impacts over mitigating radiation effects.
